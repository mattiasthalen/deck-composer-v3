# Vren, the Relentless — playbook

Deck file: `decks/vren.deck.txt`. Colours: blue-black. Bracket 2.
Every figure below is `check` output for this list; nothing was counted by hand.

## What the deck is

A removal deck that turns every answer into a Rat. While Vren is on the
battlefield, a creature an opponent controls that would die is exiled instead.
At the beginning of each end step — yours and everyone else's — you create one
1/1 black Rat token for each creature exiled from an opponent's control that
turn, and each of those tokens gets +1/+1 for every other Rat you control. Kill
spells, deathtouch blocks and exile effects all count. Bounce and tapping do
not. Vren has ward {2}, so an opponent's spell or ability that targets him is
countered unless they pay two more.

- 24 creatures. Average mana value 2.91 (window 2.5–3.2). Curve by mana value:

  not a build choice.

## How to pilot it

1. Keep a hand with three or four lands, at least one of each colour, and a
   cheap answer or a two-mana Rat. A hand of only creatures is weaker than it
   looks; this deck wins by trading one-for-one and keeping the tokens.
2. Early turns: cheap Rats and deathtouch bodies (Shoreline Looter, Azure
   Beastbinder, Persistent Marshstalker, Burrog Banemaker,
   Daggerfang Duo). Kill mana creatures on sight with Requiting Hex, Savor or
   Fell; a ramp deck that loses its first dork loses a turn.
3. Cast Vren on four mana, ideally with removal still in hand. From then on
   every removal spell is also a Rat at the next end step, and the Rats grow
   each other. Instant-speed removal cast on an opponent's turn makes its Rat
   at that same end step.
4. Hold counter mana for the spell that matters. Essence Scatter and Long
   River's Pull stop a creature; Cancel, Devious Cover-Up and Spellgyre
   stop anything. A commander that gets cheaper with the board
   (Ghalta) is best countered, because killing it only sends it back to the
   command zone.
5. Use the right answer for the threat. Destroy and exile (Hero's Downfall,
   Feed the Cycle, Fell, Nocturnal Hunger, Foolish Fate, Early Winter, Wander
   Off, Eaten Alive, Grisly Ritual, Deadly Plot) make Rats. Sugar Coat, Eaten
   by Piranhas and Pin to the Earth neutralise a creature without killing it —
   keep them for indestructible or recursive threats, above all Ghalta-style
   commanders that come back cheap. Into the Roil and Bumbleflower's Sharepot
   answer an artifact or enchantment (an equipment, an aura, a pump). Nowhere to Run lets your
   spells target hexproof creatures for the rest of the game.
6. Aetherspouts is the answer to an alpha strike: every attacking creature
   goes to the top or bottom of its owner's library. Hold five mana for it
   when the opponent's board can kill you in one swing.
7. Maha, Its Feathers Night is the closer. Every creature your opponents
   control gets base toughness 1, so each Rat token trades with anything and
   Savor or Downwind Ambusher kills outright.

## What to watch

- No sweeper. A wide token board has to be answered one creature at a time;
  the Rats and deathtouch blockers are the plan against it.
- Card draw is the thin axis. Use Diresight, Stargaze, Quick Study, Jace's
  Ingenuity and Fountainport (sacrifice a token) before the
  hand runs dry, not after.
- Vren is the engine. Keep a removal spell back rather than empty your hand
  into a board that a single answer to Vren resets.
- Rat tokens depend on each other; a sweeper or an edict on a big token costs
  more than one Rat.
- Temporary answers (Into the Flood Maw, Dire Downdraft, Starlight Snare)
  buy a turn against a big creature but do not make Rats. Spend them on
  tempo, not on the threat that ends the game.
- Vampiric Tutor is deliberately not in the deck: it is the one Game Changer
  in the collection and this table is Bracket 2.
